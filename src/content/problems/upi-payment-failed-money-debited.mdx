Nearly every page on this subject tells you to wait three to five working days
and contact your bank. That advice is both vague and worse than the rule you are
actually entitled to.

## The deadline

<Callout type="rule" title="T+1, then ₹100 a day" cite="RBI circular DPSS.CO.PD No.629/02.01.014/2019-20" citeUrl="https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=11693">
For a UPI transfer where your **account was debited but the beneficiary was not
credited**, the amount must be auto-reversed by **T+1**, where T is the
transaction date. Beyond that, the bank must pay you **₹100 per day of delay**,
credited automatically, with no claim required from you.
</Callout>

T+1 means the day after the transaction, not one working day. A failed transfer
on Saturday is due back by Sunday.

The same circular sets deadlines across every failure type:

<div class="tablewrap">

| What failed | Reversal deadline | Compensation after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UPI transfer, account debited, payee not credited | T+1 | ₹100 per day |
| IMPS or NEFT, account debited, beneficiary not credited | T+1 | ₹100 per day |
| ATM withdrawal, cash not dispensed | T+5 days | ₹100 per day |
| Card transaction at a merchant, no confirmation | T+5 days | ₹100 per day |
| Card-not-present, account debited, no confirmation | T+5 days | ₹100 per day |

</div>

## Why your bank says five working days

Because a genuinely failed transaction and a slow one look identical to you for
the first day. The call centre quotes the worst case to buy time.

What it does not tell you is that the compensation clock has already started. If
your money comes back on day four, the bank owes you ₹300 on top of it.

<Callout type="warn" title="Do not repeat the payment">
A pending UPI transaction may still settle. Paying again often means paying
twice, and recovering the second one is a far harder problem than waiting out
the first. Check the status in the app before you retry.
</Callout>

## What to do

If T+1 has passed and the money is not back, the complaint must be raised in the
right place to be useful.

1. **Raise it in the UPI app first.** Every UPI app has a built-in complaint flow
   against the specific transaction. This routes it into NPCI's dispute system
   rather than a general support queue. Note the reference number.
2. **Then your bank, in writing.** Email the grievance channel with the UPI
   transaction reference, the date, the amount, and the line that matters: that
   under circular DPSS.CO.PD No.629/02.01.014/2019-20 the reversal deadline was
   T+1 and compensation of ₹100 per day is now due.
3. **Thirty days later, the Ombudsman.** Free, online, no lawyer.
   [How to file](/disputes/rbi-ombudsman-how-to-file).

Citing the circular number changes the conversation. A generic complaint about a
delayed refund gets a templated reply. A complaint quoting the instrument the
bank is bound by gets routed to someone who knows what it is.

## When it is not a failed transaction

Two cases fall outside these rules entirely, and it is worth being clear about
them because the remedy is completely different.

**You paid the wrong person.** The transaction worked. There is a process to ask
the recipient's bank to seek their consent for a reversal, but no mechanism
compels an unwilling recipient to return the money. Act within 24 hours, raise it
in the app and with both banks, and understand that the outcome depends on
someone else's goodwill.

**The money left without you initiating anything.** That is an unauthorised
transaction, not a failed one, and the rules are far more favourable to you —
often zero liability.
[That is a different page](/fraud/unauthorised-transaction-bank-refund), and the
reporting deadline there is three working days.
