Closing a credit card should be the simplest thing a bank does. It is often the
slowest, because every day the card stays open is another chance to charge an
annual fee or sell a retention offer. RBI's 2022 master direction put a price on
that delay.

<Callout type="rule" title="Seven working days, then ₹500 a day" cite="RBI Master Direction on Credit Card and Debit Card – Issuance and Conduct Directions, 2022" citeUrl="https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12300">
A card issuer must complete the closure of a credit card **within seven working
days** of a request, provided all dues are paid. If it fails to do so, it must
pay the cardholder **₹500 per day of delay** until the account is actually
closed.
</Callout>

The same direction requires issuers to offer **at least one** channel for closure
requests that is not a phone call — email, a dedicated web page, the app, or
post — precisely because the phone queue was where closure requests used to go to
die.

## What slows it down

- **An unpaid balance.** The obligation applies once dues are cleared. Pay the
  statement in full first, including any pro-rated annual fee, then request
  closure in writing.
- **An auto-debit or standing instruction still attached.** Remove these before
  requesting closure. A card with an active mandate can bounce a payment and
  create a fresh due after you thought it was shut.
- **Reward points.** Points are usually forfeited on closure. Redeem them first;
  the bank will not remind you.
- **A credit balance.** If the bank owes you money on the card, it must transfer
  that balance to your bank account. It cannot keep it against a future dispute.

<Callout type="warn" title="Get a closure confirmation in writing">
The card being declined at a terminal does not mean the account is closed. Ask
for written confirmation of closure and, a month later, check your credit report
shows the account as **closed**. Cards that are "closed" in the branch system but
still open at the bureau are common, and they quietly damage your report.
</Callout>

## If seven working days pass

Write to the bank's grievance redressal officer. Three things belong in the
email, and nothing else:

1. The date of the original closure request and its reference number.
2. That dues were cleared as of that date.
3. That under the Master Direction on Credit Card and Debit Card – Issuance and
   Conduct Directions, 2022, closure was due within seven working days and
   compensation of ₹500 per day is now accruing.

Then, 30 days after the complaint, escalate to the RBI Ombudsman. Credit card
grievances are explicitly covered, filing is free, and the ₹500-a-day provision
is unusually easy to adjudicate because it turns on two dates.

## Before you close it

Worth a moment's thought: closing an old card shortens the average age of your
accounts and removes its limit from your total available credit, which raises
your utilisation ratio. Both feed your score. If the card is free and you simply
do not use it, keeping it open and dormant is often better for your report than
closing it — assuming the issuer does not close it for inactivity first.
