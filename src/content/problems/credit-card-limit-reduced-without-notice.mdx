A limit cut usually surfaces at the worst moment: a transaction declines, and you
find out from the merchant rather than the bank.

## What the issuer is allowed to do

Reducing a limit is within an issuer's rights. The card agreement provides for it
and it is a commercial risk decision, which means the RBI Ombudsman will not
overturn the decision itself.

What it must do is **tell you**. A change to a material term of your card has to
be communicated. An issuer that cut your limit and informed you only by declining
a transaction has a process failure to answer for, and that is a complaint the
Ombudsman will take up.

<Callout type="warn" title="The real damage is to your utilisation">
Cut a ₹3,00,000 limit to ₹1,00,000 while you carry a ₹60,000 balance and your
utilisation goes from 20% to 60% overnight. You spent nothing. Your score falls
anyway, and every lender who pulls your file sees the higher ratio.
</Callout>

[Work out what your ratio is now](/tools/credit-utilisation-calculator).

## What usually triggers it

- **Inactivity.** The most common reason by far. An unused limit is capital the issuer is holding against nothing, so dormant cards get trimmed.
- **A bureau signal.** A missed payment anywhere, a new large loan, or a run of enquiries can trigger a portfolio review across all your cards, not just the one that saw the problem.
- **High revolving usage.** Consistently paying only the minimum reads as stress, even when you never miss a payment.
- **Portfolio-level tightening.** Nothing to do with you. Issuers periodically pull back across whole segments.

Inactivity is the one you control. A small recurring charge on autopay, enough to
generate a statement, is usually sufficient.

## Getting it restored

Write to the issuer and ask for two things: the reason for the reduction, and a
review for reinstatement. Include anything that has changed in your favour —
higher income, a closed loan, cleared balances.

Restoration is not automatic and not guaranteed, but it is a routine request and
issuers do grant it, particularly if the trigger was inactivity and you have since
started using the card.

If the issuer did not notify you at all, say so explicitly and separately in the
complaint. That is the part with a remedy. Thirty days later the Ombudsman is open
to you, free, on the notification failure rather than on the decision.
[How to file](/disputes/rbi-ombudsman-how-to-file).

## Do not close the card in frustration

It is the natural reaction and it makes the utilisation problem worse, because
you lose the remaining limit too.
