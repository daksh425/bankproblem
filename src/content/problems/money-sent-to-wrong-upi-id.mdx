This is a different problem from a failed payment, and it is important to be clear
about that from the start, because the advice people find online usually conflates
the two.

Your transaction **worked**. The money went exactly where you told it to go. None
of the turn-around-time rules apply, no compensation accrues, and nothing
auto-reverses.

## What you can actually do

<Callout type="warn" title="There is no mechanism to force a return">
A successful credit to a valid account cannot be reversed without the account
holder's consent. What the system provides is a route to **ask** them. Anyone
promising a guaranteed recovery, for a fee, is selling you something that does not
exist.
</Callout>

That said, the request succeeds more often than people expect, particularly for
ordinary mistaken transfers between ordinary people. Speed is what decides it.

**Within the first hour.** Raise a complaint against the specific transaction
inside your UPI app. Every app has this against the transaction record. It routes
into the dispute system rather than a general support queue. Note the reference.

**Same day.** Write to your bank with the UPI transaction reference number, the
date, the amount and the UPI ID it went to. Ask your bank to approach the
beneficiary bank for consent to reverse. Get a complaint reference number.

**Within a few days.** If nothing has moved, escalate to the grievance redressal
officer in writing. Thirty days after that written complaint, the RBI Ombudsman
covers the handling of your complaint — not the recipient's refusal, but whether
your bank followed the process.
[How to file](/disputes/rbi-ombudsman-how-to-file).

## Why speed decides it

Money sitting in an account is far easier to return than money already spent or
moved on. After a few days the realistic recovery rate falls sharply. This is the
single reason to act the same hour rather than the next morning.

## If it was a scam, not a mistake

Completely different route, and a much better one. If you were deceived into
sending it — a fake delivery charge, an impersonated relative, a fraudulent
seller — report it on the **national cybercrime portal at cybercrime.gov.in or the
1930 helpline immediately**, as well as to your bank.

That can trigger a freeze on the beneficiary account, which is a mechanism that
does not exist for an honest typo. Reporting within the first hours materially
changes the odds.

If money left your account without you initiating anything at all, that is an
unauthorised transaction and the rules strongly favour you — often zero liability.
[The liability caps and the three-working-day deadline](/fraud/unauthorised-transaction-bank-refund).

## Avoiding the next one

Check the **name** the app displays after you enter the UPI ID, before you
confirm. It is the only verification step in the flow and it is the one everybody
skips. Send ₹1 first on any large transfer to a new payee; the rupee is cheaper
than the alternative.
