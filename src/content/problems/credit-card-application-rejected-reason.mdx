The rejection email says something like "your application does not meet our
current criteria". That sentence is deliberately empty. Working out which
criterion you missed takes a bit of elimination.

## The filters, in the order they are applied

**Eligibility gates.** Minimum age, minimum income for that specific card,
employment type, city, and whether your employer is on the issuer's approved
list. These are hard rules checked before anything else, and no score will get
you past them. A card with a ₹12 lakh income requirement will reject a ₹9 lakh
applicant with an 800 score, every time.

**Bureau check.** Score, plus the report itself. A settled account, a current
overdue, or a write-off can reject you on its own.
["Settled" is the one people miss](/credit-score/settled-vs-closed-credit-report).

**Internal checks.** If you already bank with them, your account conduct matters:
cheque returns, average balance, existing exposure. Issuers also cap how many of
their own cards one person can hold.

**Capacity.** Existing EMIs and card limits against your income. Issuers count
the **limits** you already hold, not just the balances, because an unused limit
is money you could draw tomorrow.

<Callout type="warn" title="Total existing limit is an underrated reason">
Someone with four cards and ₹8 lakh of unused limit can be rejected for a fifth
on exposure grounds alone, with a perfect record and plenty of income. If you are
collecting cards, this is the wall you eventually hit.
</Callout>

## Asking for the reason

Write to the issuer's grievance channel, not the call centre, and ask for the
specific reason for rejection in writing. Reference its fair practices code,
which commits it to conveying reasons.

You will usually get a category rather than a precise trigger — "credit bureau
records", "internal policy", "income criteria". That is still worth having,
because it tells you which of the four filters above to work on and saves you
guessing.

If the reason given is **bureau records**, pull all four reports before doing
anything else. That answer means there is something on your file, and it may not
be something you know about.

## Before you reapply

Change something real. Three to six months, and at least one of:

- An error on your report corrected, with the dispute closed.
- Card balances paid down so your utilisation is comfortably under 30%.
- A documented increase in income, updated with the issuer.
- A closed loan or surrendered limit that reduces your existing exposure.

And apply to one issuer at a time. Four applications in a fortnight turns a
single rejection into a pattern that the next issuer can see.
