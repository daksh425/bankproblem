You did the one thing that mattered: you reported fast. That puts you in the
zero-liability band for a third-party breach. A refusal at this point is usually
one of four things, and each has a specific answer.

## Establish the reporting date first

Everything turns on it. Your reporting date is **when you notified the bank**, not
when the bank got around to logging a dispute.

If you called the 24x7 helpline on day one and the formal complaint was raised on
day four, the bank may be calculating liability from day four. Produce the first
call's reference number, or the call time from your phone records, and insist the
clock runs from there.

## The four refusals and what to say

**"You were negligent."** The burden of proving that sits with the bank, and
"correct credentials were used" is not proof of your conduct.
[Why that assertion does not hold](/fraud/bank-blamed-negligence-burden-of-proof).

**"The investigation is ongoing."** Fine, and separate. The credit is due within
**10 working days of your notification** regardless. Ask for the credit now and
let the investigation run.

**"Our policy says otherwise."** A bank's board-approved policy may be more
generous than the circular, never less. Ask which clause, and point out that it
cannot reduce the protection the circular gives you.

**"It was a merchant transaction, raise a chargeback."** Sometimes legitimate, often
a deflection. If the transaction was unauthorised, the liability framework applies
whatever the channel. Ask the bank to state in writing why it considers the
transaction authorised.

<Callout type="rule" title="The 10-working-day credit is not conditional" cite="RBI circular DBR.No.Leg.BC.78/09.07.005/2017-18, para 9" citeUrl="https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=11040">
On being notified, the bank must credit the disputed amount **within 10 working
days from the date of notification**. It is reversed later only if the
investigation establishes your liability. The credit comes first; the finding
comes after.
</Callout>

## Get the refusal in writing

This is the step that changes the outcome more than any argument. Ask for the
rejection, with the specific ground, in writing.

Banks are markedly more careful about what they will commit to an email than what
a call centre will say on the phone. Many refusals quietly evaporate at this
point, and an unreasoned written rejection is strong evidence if it does not.

## Then escalate

Thirty days after your written complaint, the RBI Ombudsman. Keep the framing
narrow and factual:

- The date you reported and the reference number.
- That the loss arose from a third-party breach.
- That the bank has alleged negligence without discharging the burden the circular places on it.
- That the 10-working-day credit has not been made.

Attach the first complaint, the bank's reply, and the transaction records. Free,
online, no lawyer.
[How to file](/disputes/rbi-ombudsman-how-to-file) ·
[How to write it so it is not template-replied](/disputes/writing-a-bank-complaint-that-works).

And note the outer limit: a complaint to the Ombudsman must be filed **within one
year** of the bank's reply. Do not let a long internal argument run past it.
