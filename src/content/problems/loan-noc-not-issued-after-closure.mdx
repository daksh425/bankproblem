Paying the last EMI does not close a loan. The paperwork does, and the paperwork
is what you will need years later when you sell the property or apply for the
next loan.

## What you should be collecting

Ask for all of these, in writing, and do not accept one in place of another:

- **The No Objection Certificate.** States that the lender has no further claim against you or the security. This is the document.
- **The loan closure statement**, showing a nil balance and the closure date.
- **Original property documents**, where the loan was secured. Check every page against the list you handed over at sanction.
- **The lien or charge release.** For a vehicle loan, the hypothecation on the RC must be removed. For property, the charge registered with the sub-registrar or on CERSAI must be released.
- **Cancelled security cheques and NACH mandate cancellation**, so nothing can be presented later.

<Callout type="warn" title="Missing documents surface years later, at the worst time">
Nobody notices an unreleased charge until they try to sell the flat or take a top-up
loan. By then the branch staff have changed, the file is in storage, and what
would have taken a week now takes months. Collect everything at closure, check it
page by page, and store it together.
</Callout>

## Chasing it

Start with a written request to the branch and the loan servicing desk, quoting
the loan account number and the closure date, listing exactly what you want.
Vagueness is what lets this drift.

If nothing moves in a fortnight, escalate to the grievance redressal officer. Say
what you asked for, when, and that the loan is fully repaid. Attach the closure
statement or the final payment proof.

RBI has taken a firm line on lenders sitting on property documents after closure,
including compensating borrowers for delay. If your loan was secured on property
and the originals have not come back, say so explicitly — that is the part with
real weight behind it.

## If the bank has lost your documents

It happens, and it is the bank's problem rather than yours. Ask in writing for a
written acknowledgement that the documents are untraceable, and for the bank to
bear the cost of obtaining certified copies and of publishing the public notice
that is usually required.

Do not accept an informal verbal assurance. You will need the written
acknowledgement when you sell.

## Escalating

Thirty days after the written complaint, the RBI Ombudsman covers non-adherence to
a lender's own published timelines and fair practices code. Free, online, no
lawyer. [How to file](/disputes/rbi-ombudsman-how-to-file).

While you are at it, check your credit report. A repaid loan still showing as open
is extremely common and it eats into your apparent capacity for the next loan.
[Getting that fixed](/loans/loan-account-still-open-on-credit-report).
