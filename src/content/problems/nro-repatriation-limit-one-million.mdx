Getting money out of an NRO account is routine, but it is a documented process
rather than a transfer, and people are caught out by discovering that the day
they need the money.

## The cap

<Callout type="rule" title="USD 1 million per financial year, per person">
A non-resident may remit up to **USD 1 million per financial year** from their NRO
account balances, covering sale proceeds of assets and other eligible
capital-account items. The limit applies **per person across all NRO accounts**,
not per account, and the financial year runs April to March.
</Callout>

Current income — rent, dividends, interest, pension — on which Indian tax has been
paid is generally treated as repatriable without eating into the cap. The cap
bites on capital items, of which property sale proceeds are the big one.

## The two forms

This is the part that surprises people.

**Form 15CA** is your own declaration about the remittance, filed online on the
income-tax portal.

**Form 15CB** is a certificate from a **chartered accountant** confirming the
nature of the remittance and that the applicable Indian taxes have been paid or
provided for.

Your bank will not process the transfer without these where they are required.
Budget time and a professional fee for it, and engage the CA before you need the
money rather than after.

## What the bank will want to see

The documentation is about proving where the money came from:

- Evidence of the **source of funds** — the sale deed, the rent agreement, the dividend statement, the inheritance documents.
- Proof that **Indian tax has been paid**, which the 15CB speaks to.
- Your **passport, visa and overseas address proof**.
- The bank's own request form, often called an A2 form.

Thin documentation is the single biggest cause of delay. An NRO account with clean
credits, each traceable to an identified source, moves quickly. One with mixed and
unexplained credits does not — which is the practical argument for keeping NRE and
NRO money strictly separate.
[Why the source decides the account](/nri/nre-vs-nro-account-which-one).

## Property sale proceeds

The most common large repatriation and the one with the most conditions attached.
How the property was acquired — purchased, inherited, gifted — changes what you
must show and sometimes what you may send.

[Selling inherited property as an NRI](/nri/selling-inherited-property-as-nri)
covers it separately, because the TDS position alone is worth a page.

## Plan around the financial year

The cap resets on 1 April. A large repatriation can sometimes be split across two
financial years to stay within it, but that is a decision to take with your CA
before the sale, not after the money is sitting in the account.

And if your old resident savings account was never redesignated as NRO, sort that
first. Repatriating from an account you were not entitled to hold in that form is
a problem you do not want to discover at the point of transfer.
[How to redesignate it](/nri/resident-account-after-moving-abroad).
