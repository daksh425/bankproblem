"Your application could not be processed at this time" is not a reason. It is a
sentence written to avoid giving one.

You cannot compel a bank to lend. You can reasonably expect it to follow the
process it published.

## Why banks are vague

Partly caution: a specific reason invites an argument, and occasionally a
discrimination claim. Partly that the decision was made by a scorecard nobody at
the branch can interrogate. And partly habit.

Most lenders' fair practices codes commit them to conveying the reason for
rejection. The commitment exists; it is just not volunteered.

## Asking so that you get an answer

Write to the grievance redressal officer, not the branch and not the call centre.
Keep it to one page and ask for three specific things:

1. The **reason for rejection**, referencing the lender's fair practices code commitment to convey it.
2. Which **credit bureau** was used, and the date of the enquiry.
3. Whether the decision was **policy-based or bureau-based**.

That third question is the useful one. The answer is usually a category rather
than the precise trigger, and a category is enough: it tells you whether to work
on your report, your income documentation, or your existing obligations.

<Callout type="rule" title="You are entitled to know what they read about you">
Whatever the lender says about its own policy, you can always pull your own credit
reports from all four bureaus directly. If the rejection was bureau-based, the
answer is sitting in a report you can obtain yourself, free, once a year from each.
</Callout>

## What you will probably find

In most cases the reason is not the score.
[Five things that reject a 780 applicant](/credit-score/loan-rejected-despite-good-credit-score)
covers the usual suspects: obligations already too high against income, a thin
file, a cluster of recent enquiries, an employer or pincode on an internal list,
or something on the report that is not yours.

The last one is worth ruling out before anything else, because it is both common
and correctable.
[How to dispute a credit report entry](/credit-score/credit-report-error-dispute).

## Escalating

Thirty days after the written complaint, the RBI Ombudsman will take up
non-adherence to a published fair practices code. Be precise about what you are
complaining about: not that you were refused, but that the lender declined to
convey the reason its own code says it will convey.

Framed that way it is a process complaint and admissible. Framed as "they should
have given me the loan" it will be rejected as commercial judgement.
