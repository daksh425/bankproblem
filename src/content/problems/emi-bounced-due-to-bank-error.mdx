The money was there. The mandate was live. The debit failed anyway, and now there
are charges from two directions and a worrying entry on your loan account.

The charges are the small problem. The credit report entry is the one to deal
with first.

## Establish what actually happened

Pull the account statement for that date and check the balance at the time the
presentation was attempted, not at the end of the day. Debits are presented in
batches, often early morning, and a balance that arrived at 2pm was not there when
the mandate ran.

Then look at the **reason code**. Your bank or the lender can tell you why the
mandate failed. The common ones that are not your fault:

- The mandate was never registered properly, or had expired.
- A technical or network failure at the sponsor bank.
- A debit freeze or hold placed on the account for an unrelated reason.
- The mandate amount did not match a revised EMI after a rate change.

<Callout type="warn" title="Ask for the reason code in writing">
"Insufficient funds" is the default label that gets applied to a lot of failures
that were nothing of the sort. A written reason code, with the presentation
timestamp, is what turns this from your word against theirs into a documented
bank-side failure.
</Callout>

## Getting the charges reversed

Write to both — the lender and your own bank — with the statement extract showing
the balance, the timestamp of the attempted debit, and the reason code. Ask for
reversal of the bounce charge and any penal interest.

Where the failure was on the bank's side, this is usually granted without much
argument. Where it was a mandate registration problem, the party that failed to
register it correctly owns the consequence.

## The part that matters more

Ask explicitly, in the same letter, for confirmation that **the bounce will not be
reported to the credit bureaus as a missed payment**, and if it already has been,
for it to be withdrawn.

People focus on the ₹500 and let the reporting go. A missed-payment marker on a
loan account affects your borrowing for years; the charge is a one-off. Make the
reporting the headline of your complaint.

If the marker is already on your report, dispute it with the bureau and the lender
in parallel, attaching the statement and the reason code. They must resolve it in
30 days, and owe you ₹100 a day beyond that.
[How to file the dispute properly](/credit-score/credit-report-error-dispute).

## Preventing the next one

Keep a buffer that covers the EMI from the day before the due date, not the day
of. Check the mandate is still registered after any rate change that altered the
instalment, because a revised EMI above the mandate's cap will simply fail.

And if a mandate you cancelled is still being presented, that is a different
problem with a cleaner remedy.
[Stopping a standing instruction the bank keeps honouring](/payments/standing-instruction-not-cancelled).
