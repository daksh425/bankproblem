Most people who move abroad keep using the same savings account, the same debit
card and the same UPI. Nothing breaks, so nothing prompts a change.

It is still not permitted, and the longer it runs the messier it is to unwind.

## What is supposed to happen

<Callout type="rule" title="Redesignate, do not abandon">
On becoming a person resident outside India, your existing **resident savings
account must be redesignated as an NRO account**. The obligation to inform the
bank of the change in residential status sits with you, not with the bank.
</Callout>

The NRO account holds and manages your **Indian-source income** — rent, dividends,
interest, a pension, proceeds from a property sale. It is the account your Indian
money lives in once you are non-resident.

It is not the same thing as an NRE account, and the difference matters a great
deal for tax and for getting money out.
[NRE vs NRO, and which one each type of money belongs in](/nri/nre-vs-nro-account-which-one).

## Why leaving it alone causes problems

**It is a FEMA non-compliance**, and the exposure is yours rather than the bank's.

**Interest is taxed differently.** Interest on an NRO account is subject to tax
deduction at source at the non-resident rate. Interest credited to a resident
account you were not entitled to hold does not become tax-free because of the
label.

**Repatriation gets harder.** Money sitting in an account that should have been
NRO, with no clean record of its source, is much more difficult to send abroad
later. The bank will want to see where each credit came from.

**The account may simply be frozen.** Typically at the next re-KYC cycle, when the
bank notices a foreign address or a mismatch. That is a bad moment for it to
happen, because you are not in the country.
[What to do when it is frozen from abroad](/nri/nri-account-frozen-rekyc-from-abroad).

## Converting it

Write to the branch asking for redesignation of the account from resident to NRO
with effect from the date your status changed. Expect to provide:

- Passport and visa or residence permit.
- Proof of overseas address.
- Your PAN.
- The bank's own NRO account forms.

Most banks handle this for existing customers without requiring you to be present
in India, though they will usually want documents attested — by the Indian
embassy, a notary, or an overseas branch of the same bank.

Do the same for your **demat account, mutual fund folios and insurance policies**,
which have parallel requirements and are even more commonly overlooked.

## While you are at it

Update the nomination on every account. And if you intend to send foreign earnings
to India, open an **NRE** account too: it is the one that keeps the money freely
repatriable and the interest tax-free in India.
