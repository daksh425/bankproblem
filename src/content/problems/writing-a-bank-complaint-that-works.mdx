Bank complaints get template replies because most of them are written as
grievances rather than as claims. A grievance describes how badly you were
treated. A claim states a rule, a date, and a remedy.

The second kind gets routed to someone who can act.

## The five things that must be in it

1. **Your account or card number and the exact transaction reference.** Put it at the top, not buried in paragraph three.
2. **Dates.** When it happened, when you first reported it, and the reference number you were given. Dates are what every deadline runs from.
3. **The rule.** Name the circular, master direction or scheme if there is one. You do not need to quote it at length; the number alone changes who reads the file.
4. **What you want.** A specific, numbered remedy: reverse this charge, credit this amount, correct this entry, issue this document.
5. **A deadline.** State that you will escalate to the RBI Ombudsman if unresolved in 30 days. That is not a threat, it is the actual scheme requirement, and it signals you know the process.

<Callout type="rule" title="Naming the instrument is the single highest-value line">
"My refund is late" gets a template. "Under RBI circular DPSS.CO.PD
No.629/02.01.014/2019-20 the reversal was due on T+1 and compensation of ₹100 per
day is now accruing" does not, because the first sentence can be answered by a
script and the second cannot.
</Callout>

Several pages on this site give you the circular number for exactly this reason.

## What to leave out

- **How the call centre spoke to you.** It is real and it is irrelevant to the remedy. It dilutes the claim.
- **Threats of social media or legal action.** They change nothing and they make the file easier to dismiss.
- **Long background.** The reader does not need your history with the bank; they need the account number and the date.
- **Several unrelated problems in one letter.** File separately. Bundling guarantees that one gets answered and the rest vanish.

## A structure to copy

> **Subject:** Complaint — [one line] — A/c [number] — [date of incident]
>
> **What happened.** Two or three sentences. Dates, amounts, reference numbers.
>
> **What I have already done.** First report on [date], reference [number]. Response received on [date], or none received.
>
> **The applicable rule.** [Circular or scheme name and number, one sentence on what it requires.]
>
> **What I am asking for.** 1. [Remedy]. 2. [Any compensation due]. 3. A written response.
>
> **Escalation.** If this is unresolved within 30 days I will file with the RBI Ombudsman under the Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2021.

Attach the statement extract, the earlier correspondence, and anything the bank
put in writing. Nothing else.

## Where to send it

Not the branch and not the call centre. The grievance redressal officer, then the
nodal officer.
[How to find the right one for your bank](/disputes/finding-bank-nodal-officer).

Keep every acknowledgement. Thirty days from the first written complaint is when
the Ombudsman opens to you, and you will need to prove that date.
[How to file](/disputes/rbi-ombudsman-how-to-file).
