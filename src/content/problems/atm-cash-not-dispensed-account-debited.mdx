This one has a clear deadline and automatic compensation, and almost nobody
claims it.

## The rule

<Callout type="rule" title="T+5 days, then ₹100 for every day late" cite="RBI circular DPSS.CO.PD No.629/02.01.014/2019-20, 20 September 2019" citeUrl="https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=11693">
Where an ATM transaction fails and cash is not dispensed but the account is
debited, the amount must be reversed **within T+5 calendar days**, T being the
transaction date. Beyond that, the bank must pay **₹100 per day of delay**,
credited **automatically**, without any claim from you.
</Callout>

In practice most of these reverse on their own within a couple of days. The rule
matters for the ones that do not.

## What to do on the spot

- **Keep the slip** if the machine printed one, including a failure slip. Photograph it.
- **Note the ATM ID**, usually on a sticker near the screen, plus the location, date and time.
- **Check the account immediately** in your app and screenshot the debit entry.
- **Do not retry** at the same machine for the same amount. If both transactions are later treated as separate events you have doubled the problem.

## Reporting it

Report to **your own card-issuing bank**, not the bank whose ATM it was. The
obligation is on the issuer and it settles with the ATM's owner separately.

Use a channel that gives you a reference number, then follow up in writing with
the date, time, amount, ATM ID and that reference. If T+5 has passed, add one
line: that under circular DPSS.CO.PD No.629/02.01.014/2019-20 the reversal was due
within five days and compensation of ₹100 per day is now accruing.

That sentence is what moves the file off the standard script.
[Work out exactly what is owed](/tools/bank-compensation-calculator).

## If the bank says the transaction was successful

Banks rely on the ATM's electronic journal and the end-of-day cash balance at the
machine. Where the journal says cash was dispensed, you will be told the
transaction succeeded.

Ask, in writing, for the **ATM's electronic journal extract and the cash
reconciliation** for that machine on that date. A machine that dispensed cash it
did not record shows up as an excess at reconciliation. You are entitled to have
the matter properly investigated rather than closed on a one-line assertion.

Thirty days after the written complaint, the Ombudsman is open, free, and this is
exactly the kind of documented, date-driven dispute it handles well.
[How to file](/disputes/rbi-ombudsman-how-to-file).
