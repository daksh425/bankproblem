Minimum balance penalties are among the most-complained-about bank charges in
India, and among the least understood. Banks are allowed to levy them. They are
not allowed to levy them the way many customers experience it.

## The three limits

<Callout type="rule" title="Notice, proportionality, and no negative balance" cite="RBI circular on penal charges for non-maintenance of minimum balance, 20 November 2014" citeUrl="https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=9321">
**Notice.** The bank must inform you that the balance has fallen below the
required level and that a penalty will apply if it is not restored, and allow a
**reasonable period, of at least one month**, to restore it.

**Proportionality.** The charge must be **proportionate to the shortfall**. A flat
penalty that bears no relation to how far below the requirement you fell does not
meet this.

**No negative balance.** Penal charges must **not result in the balance turning
negative** purely on account of those charges.
</Callout>

Those three together rule out the common pattern people complain about: an account
quietly drained by repeated flat penalties, with no notice, until it goes into
the red.

## The option nobody offers you

Every bank must offer a **Basic Savings Bank Deposit account**, which has **no
minimum balance requirement** and includes a basic set of free services — a debit
card, and a limited number of free withdrawals a month.

You can ask for your existing savings account to be converted to a BSBD account.
Branches rarely volunteer this, because the account earns the bank less.

<Callout type="ok" title="If the minimum balance is the problem, change the account">
For someone who keeps a low balance and uses the account mainly to receive and
spend, a BSBD account removes the entire issue permanently. Ask for it in writing
if the branch is unhelpful.
</Callout>

The trade-off is real: BSBD accounts limit free withdrawals and some services. Read
what you give up before converting.

## Getting a charge reversed

Pull the statement and identify each penalty by date and amount. Then check:

- Did you receive any **notice** before the first penalty, and were you given a month?
- Is the amount **proportionate** to how far below you actually were, or is it a flat figure?
- Did the charges push the balance **below zero**?
- Is the charge in the bank's **published schedule of charges** that you agreed to?

Write to the grievance redressal officer listing the dates and amounts, and which
of the above the bank failed on. Ask for reversal and for the account to be
converted to BSBD if that is what you want going forward.

Keep it to one page.
[The structure that gets a real reply](/disputes/writing-a-bank-complaint-that-works).

## If the bank refuses

Thirty days after the written complaint, the RBI Ombudsman is open, free and
online. Charges levied contrary to the regulator's instructions or to the bank's
own published schedule sit squarely within the scheme.
