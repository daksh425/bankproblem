"Customer negligence" is the sentence that ends most fraud claims. It is worth
understanding exactly what it means and who has to establish it, because the
answer is not what the call centre implies.

## What the circular actually says

<Callout type="rule" title="The burden rests with the bank" cite="RBI circular DBR.No.Leg.BC.78/09.07.005/2017-18, 6 July 2017" citeUrl="https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=11040">
Where a customer disputes an electronic transaction, **the burden of proving
customer liability rests with the bank**. Negligence is something the bank must
establish, not something you must disprove.
</Callout>

There are three liability situations, and only one of them is about your conduct:

- **Bank-side deficiency** — zero liability, whenever you report.
- **Third-party breach, nobody at fault** — zero liability if reported within three working days, capped between ₹5,000 and ₹25,000 if reported within four to seven.
- **Customer negligence** — you bear the loss, but **only until you report it**.

## Asserting is not proving

In practice banks conflate two different statements:

> "Our logs show the correct credentials were used."

> "The customer shared the credentials."

The first is a fact about the bank's systems. The second is a claim about your
behaviour, and it does not follow from the first. Credentials can be intercepted
through a SIM swap, a compromised device, a hostile app, or a breach at a merchant
— none of which is you being careless.

When a bank gives you the first sentence as though it establishes the second, say
so in writing. Ask what evidence it relies on to establish negligence specifically.

<Callout type="warn" title="The part banks never mention">
Even where negligence is established, your liability **stops at the moment you
report**. Everything after that is the bank's, whatever happened before. If
transactions continued after your complaint reference was issued, those are not
arguable.
</Callout>

## What to put in writing

- The **date and time you reported**, and the reference number.
- A request for the **specific basis** on which negligence is alleged.
- A request for the **transaction logs**, including device, IP and channel for each disputed transaction.
- A reminder that under the circular the burden of proof sits with the bank.
- A separate demand for the **10-working-day credit**, which is due on notification regardless of where the investigation lands.

Keep it to a page.

## Escalating

Thirty days after the written complaint, the Ombudsman. Frame it precisely: not
that the bank was unfair, but that it has alleged negligence without discharging
the burden the circular places on it, and has withheld the credit the circular
requires within 10 working days.

That framing is admissible and adjudicable.
