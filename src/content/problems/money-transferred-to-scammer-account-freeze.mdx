This is the hardest category on this site to be honest about, so: recovery is
possible, it is far from guaranteed, and it depends almost entirely on how fast
you report.

## Why this is not an "unauthorised transaction"

The RBI liability framework covers transactions made **without your involvement** —
a cloned card, a hijacked session, a stolen credential. Your zero-liability
protection lives there.

A transfer you were persuaded to make is different. You approved it. The deception
was real and the loss is real, but the transaction itself was authorised, and the
banking rules that produce an automatic refund do not apply.

<Callout type="alert" title="Report on 1930 or at cybercrime.gov.in within the hour">
This is the mechanism that can actually stop the money. The national cybercrime
reporting system can place a hold on funds still sitting in the beneficiary
account. Once the money is withdrawn or layered onward, that window closes, and
it closes fast.
</Callout>

## The order of operations

1. **Call 1930, or file at cybercrime.gov.in.** First. Before the bank, before anything. Have the transaction reference, the amount, the beneficiary details and the time ready.
2. **Call your bank's 24x7 helpline**, report it, get a reference number, and ask them to raise it with the beneficiary bank.
3. **Preserve everything.** Screenshots of the chat or messages, the caller's number, the UPI ID or account number, any website or app you were directed to. Do not delete the conversation in embarrassment — it is the evidence.
4. **File the written complaint with the bank** the same day, referencing the cybercrime acknowledgement number.
5. **Follow up with the investigating officer** once the complaint is assigned. The bank cannot release a hold or trace further without that process.

## This changes on 1 January 2027

Worth knowing, because it narrows the gap described above.

RBI's Amendment Directions of 24 June 2026 replace "unauthorised transaction"
with a wider concept of a **fraudulent electronic banking transaction**, defined
to include transactions **approved by the customer under coercion or duress**.
Some deception-based transfers therefore come inside the customer-protection
framework for the first time.

They also create a compensation route for smaller losses: an individual losing up
to ₹50,000 may receive **85% of the net loss or ₹25,000, whichever is lower**,
where the fraud is reported **both** to the bank **and** through the National
Cyber Crime Reporting Portal or 1930, **within five calendar days**.

<Callout type="warn" title="It applies to transactions from 1 January 2027, not today">
If this happened to you now, the position in the rest of this page is the one
that governs. The practical takeaway is the same either way: report to
cybercrime.gov.in or 1930 **and** to your bank, fast. From 2027 that dual report
within five days becomes the condition for a specific entitlement.
</Callout>

## Where it sometimes becomes a bank matter

Two situations shift this back towards the bank:

**The deception involved your credentials being intercepted.** If, part way
through, an OTP was used that you did not approve, that portion may be an
unauthorised transaction after all.
[The liability rules there](/fraud/unauthorised-transaction-bank-refund).

**The bank did not act on your report.** The outcome is not the bank's fault, but
the handling can be. If you reported promptly and the bank took days to contact
the beneficiary bank, that delay is a service failure the Ombudsman will examine.
[How to file](/disputes/rbi-ombudsman-how-to-file).

## Things that make it worse

**Recovery agents.** Anyone who contacts you offering to recover scammed money for
a fee is running the second half of the same scam. This is routine — fraud victim
lists get resold. No legitimate service charges you upfront to recover funds.

**Paying more to release the funds.** A demand for a fee, tax or clearance charge
to unlock money you are owed is always fraud.

And if the transfer was simply a typo rather than a deception, the process is
different and gentler.
