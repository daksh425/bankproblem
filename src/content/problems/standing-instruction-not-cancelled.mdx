There are two separate things here, and banks sometimes blur them: cancelling the
underlying service, and withdrawing the payment mandate sitting on your account.

Only the second one stops the money leaving.

## Withdraw it at the bank

Go to net banking or the app and look for **manage mandates**, **standing
instructions**, or **e-mandates**. Registered NACH and e-mandates are listed there
and can usually be revoked directly.

If you cannot find it, write to the bank — email the grievance channel, not just
a call — stating the mandate reference or the merchant name, the amount and the
frequency, and instructing the bank to withdraw it with effect from today. Insist
on a service request number.

<Callout type="rule" title="A mandate is an instruction you gave, and you can take it back">
The debit happens because you authorised it. Withdrawing that authorisation is
your right, and once withdrawn the bank has no mandate to act on. A bank that
continues to honour a cancelled mandate is acting without authority.
</Callout>

Tell the merchant too, separately and in writing, so there is no later argument
about whether the service was cancelled.

## Debits taken after you cancelled

This is where the position becomes much stronger than people realise. A debit
taken after a valid withdrawal is not a billing dispute with the merchant. It is
an **unauthorised transaction on your account**.

Report it to the bank promptly and in writing, quoting the date you withdrew the
mandate and the service request number. Reporting within **three working days** of
noticing is the difference between zero liability and a capped loss, and the bank
must credit the disputed amount within 10 working days of your report.

[The full liability rules](/fraud/unauthorised-transaction-bank-refund).

## Things that make it worse

**Do not close the account to stop a debit.** It creates a new set of problems and
does not reliably stop a presentation that is already in flight.

**Do not simply let the balance run dry.** The mandate fails, you collect a bounce
charge from both sides, and if it is attached to anything credit-related the
failure can be reported.
[What to do about a bounce that was not your fault](/loans/emi-bounced-due-to-bank-error).

**Do not rely on cancelling in the merchant's app alone.** That is the step most
people stop at, and it is the one that most often leaves the mandate live.
[The same problem on a credit card](/cards/auto-debit-continues-after-cancellation).

## If it keeps happening

Written complaint to the grievance redressal officer, with the dates of the
withdrawal and every subsequent debit listed. Ask for reversal of each and
confirmation that the mandate has been removed.

Thirty days later, the Ombudsman.
