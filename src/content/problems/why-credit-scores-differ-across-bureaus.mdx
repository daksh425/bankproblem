India has four licensed credit bureaus: TransUnion CIBIL, Experian, Equifax and
CRIF High Mark. People assume they are four copies of one database. They are not.
Each builds its own file, from whatever its member lenders chose to send it, and
scores it with its own model.

Different numbers are the normal outcome. The question is how different.

## The three reasons the numbers diverge

**Not every lender reports to every bureau.** Reporting to the bureaus is
required, but the operational reality is that lenders have different membership
and different upload schedules. A small co-operative bank loan or an NBFC
consumer-durable loan may appear on two reports and not the other two.

**The models are different.** CIBIL's score and Experian's score are separately
built products. Both run roughly 300 to 900 and both weigh repayment history
most heavily, but how they treat a short credit history, a high-utilisation card
or a recent enquiry is proprietary and not identical.

**The snapshots are taken at different times.** Lenders upload monthly, on their
own cycle. If you cleared a large card balance on the 10th, one bureau may have
that and another may still be holding last month's figure. A gap that looks
alarming in the first week of a month often closes by the third.

<Callout type="ok" title="A 30-50 point spread is unremarkable">
Four models reading slightly different data will not agree. Treat the spread as
noise unless it is large, and compare the **accounts listed** rather than the
headline numbers.
</Callout>

## When the gap is a real problem

Stop comparing scores and start comparing account lists. Open all four reports
side by side and check:

- Does every report list the same number of loans and cards?
- Is any account marked **settled** on one report and **closed** on another? These mean very different things to a lender, and only one of them is good.
- Is a closed loan still showing as open anywhere?
- Does any report carry an account, address or employer you do not recognise?

A difference in the account list is a real error. A difference in the score with
identical account lists is just two models disagreeing, and there is nothing to
fix.

## Which one matters

Whichever one your lender pulls, and you will not know in advance. Some pull two
and take the lower. This is the practical argument against trying to optimise a
single score: the one you monitor on a free app may not be the one that decides
your application.

The useful habit is to pull all four once a year, read the account lists rather
than the numbers, and dispute anything factually wrong.
[Filing a dispute correctly is mostly about documentation](/credit-score/credit-report-error-dispute),
and since April 2024 the bureaus owe you ₹100 a day if they miss the 30-day
deadline.

the score was probably never the reason.
