It feels backwards. You removed a credit line you were not using, which ought to
look responsible, and the score went down. Two separate things cause it.

## Your utilisation ratio jumped

Utilisation is your reported balances divided by your total limits. Closing a
card removes its limit from the denominator while your balances stay exactly
where they were.

A simple case: two cards, ₹2,00,000 and ₹1,00,000 of limit, carrying ₹60,000 in
total. That is 20% utilisation. Close the ₹1,00,000 card and the same ₹60,000 is
now 30% of ₹2,00,000. You spent nothing extra and your ratio rose by half.

<Callout type="rule" title="Utilisation is read from your statement date">
It is calculated from the balance your lender reports, which is the statement
balance, not what you owe today. Paying in full before the **statement
generates** is what moves the number. Paying before the due date clears the debt
but the higher figure has already been reported.
</Callout>

[Work out your own ratio, per card and overall](/tools/credit-utilisation-calculator) —
note that a single maxed card hurts even when the overall figure looks fine.

## Your average account age fell

Length of credit history is a smaller but real factor. If the card you closed was
your oldest, closing it can shorten the average age of your accounts and take the
score with it.

This is why closing a first card from a decade ago is more costly than closing
one opened last year, even when both are unused.

## So should you keep it open?

If the card is free, generally yes. A dormant no-fee card is a quiet asset: it
adds limit, it ages, and it costs nothing. Put a small recurring charge on it so
the issuer does not close it for inactivity, and set that to autopay.

Close it when you are paying an annual fee for a card you do not use, when you
cannot keep it physically secure, or when having it available leads you to spend.
Those are good reasons and the few points are worth paying.

What is not a good reason: closing cards to "tidy up" your report. Lenders do not
reward a short list of accounts.

## If you do close it

Clear the balance, remove any standing instructions, redeem the points, and get
written confirmation of closure. Then check your report a month later.

The issuer has **seven working days** to complete the closure and owes you ₹500 a
day if it misses that.
