Dormancy sounds alarming and is mostly administrative. It is a control against
fraud on accounts nobody is watching, not a penalty.

## How an account becomes inoperative

No **customer-induced** transaction for two years. The word customer-induced is
what trips people up: interest credited by the bank, and charges debited by the
bank, do not count. An account can sit for years receiving interest and still tip
into dormancy because you never moved any money yourself.

Banks are expected to warn you before it happens, usually by SMS and email to the
contacts on record — which are often exactly the details that have gone stale on a
forgotten account.

<Callout type="ok" title="Your money is safe and still earning">
Dormancy restricts transactions. It does not touch your balance, and interest
keeps accruing on a savings account throughout. Nothing is forfeited and there is
no deadline you can miss.
</Callout>

## Reactivating it

Go to the branch where the account is held, with:

- A written request for reactivation.
- **Fresh KYC** — identity and current address proof. Expect to redo this even if nothing has changed, because the documents on file are now old.
- A passbook or an old statement if you have one.

Some banks allow it through the app or net banking where your KYC is already
current; most still want the branch for an account that has been inoperative for
years.

It should be free. RBI's position is that banks should not charge for
reactivation.

Once reactivated, make a small customer-initiated transaction — transfer ₹100 out
and back — so the clock restarts. A reactivated account with no activity simply
goes dormant again.

## If the branch is difficult

The usual friction is a demand for documents beyond standard KYC, or a long delay.
Put the request in writing, ask what specifically is outstanding, and escalate to
the grievance redressal officer after a week.

Thirty days from the written complaint, the Ombudsman covers a bank's failure to
follow its own process.
[How to file](/disputes/rbi-ombudsman-how-to-file).

## If you would rather close it

Reasonable, for an account you do not need. Note two things first: close it
properly rather than letting it lapse, and clear any minimum balance penalties
that may have accrued before dormancy set in.

## Unclaimed deposits

If an account stays inoperative for ten years the balance is transferred to RBI's
Depositor Education and Awareness Fund. Even then the money is not lost — you, or
your heirs, can claim it back from the bank, which recovers it from the fund. RBI
also runs a portal for tracing unclaimed deposits across banks.
