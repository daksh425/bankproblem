A freeze is not one thing. The first and most important step is finding out which
kind you have, because the bank can lift some of them in an afternoon and others
not at all.

## Find out who imposed it

Ask the branch, in writing, for three specific things:

1. The **nature** of the freeze — full debit freeze, partial, or credit-only.
2. **Who imposed it** — the bank itself, or an external authority.
3. If external, the **reference number and the authority's details**.

<Callout type="warn" title="The bank cannot lift a freeze it did not impose">
If a court, a tax authority or a police cyber cell ordered it, the bank has no
discretion. Spending weeks escalating within the bank achieves nothing. What you
need from the bank is the **reference and the name of the authority**, so you can
go to the right place.
</Callout>

## The common causes

**Incomplete re-KYC.** The most frequent, and the most easily fixed. Banks
periodically require updated identity and address documents. Submit them and the
account reopens, usually within a few working days.

**Dormancy.** An account with no customer-initiated transaction for two years
becomes inoperative. This is not really a freeze and reactivation is
straightforward.
[How to reactivate it](/accounts/dormant-account-reactivation).

**A cyber-crime complaint.** If someone reported a fraud and money traced through
your account — even if you were an innocent intermediary, such as a seller who
received payment from a compromised account — the investigating police station can
have the account or an amount frozen. Only that station can release it. Get the
complaint reference from the bank and approach them directly.

**A court or tax order.** Attachment orders, recovery proceedings. The route is
through that proceeding, not the bank.

**The bank's own risk flag.** Unusual transaction patterns, a mismatch between
declared and actual activity, or a name match on a screening list. These the bank
can and should resolve, and they are what the grievance process is for.

## Pressing it

Where the freeze is the bank's own, write to the grievance redressal officer. Ask
for the reason, what you must do to resolve it, and a timeline. Keep it to one
page and attach your KYC documents if that is the likely cause.

Thirty days on, the Ombudsman will take up a bank's failure to act on its own
process or to tell you what is required.
[How to file](/disputes/rbi-ombudsman-how-to-file).

It will not lift a court-ordered or police-ordered freeze. Nothing can, except the
authority that ordered it.

## While it lasts

Move salary credits and standing instructions to another account quickly. A frozen
account will bounce EMIs and mandates, and those failures get reported even though
the freeze was not your doing.
[What to do about a bounce that was not your fault](/loans/emi-bounced-due-to-bank-error).
