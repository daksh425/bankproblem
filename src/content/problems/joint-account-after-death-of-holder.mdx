This is a distressing thing to deal with at a distressing time, and banks make it
harder than it needs to be. Almost everything turns on one line in the account
opening form.

## Find the operating mandate

**Either or Survivor.** Either holder could operate it while both were alive, and
on the death of one, the survivor continues. This is the most common mandate on a
couple's account and the simplest outcome.

**Former or Survivor.** Only the first-named holder could operate it; on their
death the second named becomes the survivor and may operate it.

**Jointly.** Both signatures were required. On the death of one, the account
cannot be operated in the ordinary way, and the balance is dealt with through
nomination or succession.

The mandate is on the account opening form and usually on the passbook or
statement header. Establish it before you do anything else, because it determines
everything that follows.

<Callout type="rule" title="With survivorship, the bank should not ask for a succession certificate">
Where the mandate provides for survivorship, the survivor is entitled to the
balance without producing a succession certificate, a will, or an indemnity from
the other heirs. RBI has repeatedly told banks not to demand those documents in
this situation. If a branch is insisting, that is the point to escalate.
</Callout>

## What the bank may ask for

- The **death certificate**, original for sighting and a copy to retain.
- A **written application** from the survivor or the nominee.
- **KYC** of the person claiming.
- The deceased's unused **cheque leaves and debit card**.

That is normally the whole list where survivorship or nomination applies.

## Where there is no survivorship and no nomination

This is the hard case. The balance forms part of the estate and the bank will
require proof of entitlement — a succession certificate, letters of
administration, or probate, depending on the amount and the state.

Banks have a simplified procedure for small balances, typically involving an
indemnity and a declaration rather than a court document. Ask for the bank's
settlement-of-claims policy for deceased depositors and what its threshold is.
Every bank must have one published.

## Practical steps in the first weeks

Stop standing instructions and mandates on the account. Tell the bank which
credits are still expected. If a loan or credit card was linked, raise that
separately rather than assuming the account closure handles it.

And check the nomination on **your own** accounts while this is fresh. The
difficulty above is almost always a nomination that was never made.

## If the branch will not move

Written complaint to the grievance redressal officer, stating the mandate on the
account and that survivorship applies, and asking what specifically is
outstanding. Then the Ombudsman at 30 days — settlement of a deceased depositor's
claim is squarely within the scheme.
