Paying off a loan early should be the one transaction a bank cannot complicate.
In practice people are quoted a foreclosure charge of two to four per cent of the
outstanding amount, pay it, and never learn that on most home loans it was not
owed.

## The rule

<Callout type="rule" title="No foreclosure charge on a floating-rate loan to an individual" cite="RBI circular on levy of foreclosure charges on floating rate term loans, 5 June 2012" citeUrl="https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=7405">
Banks **shall not** levy foreclosure charges or pre-payment penalties on
**floating-rate term loans sanctioned to individual borrowers**. The reasoning
given at the time was simple: a floating-rate loan already passes interest-rate
risk to the borrower, so the lender has no rate risk to be compensated for when
the borrower leaves.
</Callout>

Most home loans in India are floating-rate loans to individuals. So are most
floating-rate personal and education loans.

## Where the protection ends

Three cases fall outside it, and banks are entitled to charge in all three:

- **Fixed-rate loans.** The lender does carry rate risk here, and the charge is contractual. Read the sanction letter for the percentage and any lock-in.
- **Loans to non-individuals.** A loan to a company, partnership or LLP is not covered, whatever the rate.
- **Loans for business purposes.** The protection is drafted around individual borrowers; a loan taken by an individual expressly for business can be treated differently. If your loan was sanctioned for a business purpose, check the specific terms.

A dual-rate loan that was fixed for an initial period and has since converted to
floating is worth arguing about: once it is floating, the usual position is that
the prohibition applies.

<Callout type="warn" title="Check what the sanction letter says the loan is">
Borrowers often do not know whether their loan is fixed or floating, and the bank
quoting the charge has no incentive to clarify. The sanction letter and the latest
statement both state the rate type. Establish that first; the whole argument turns
on it.
</Callout>

## What to do when you are quoted a charge

Ask for the foreclosure quotation **in writing**, itemised. A written quotation
showing a foreclosure charge on a floating-rate individual loan is the only
evidence you need, and banks are noticeably more careful about what they will put
in an email than what a branch will say across a desk.

Then write to the grievance redressal officer with three lines: the loan account
number, that the loan is a floating-rate term loan sanctioned to you as an
individual, and that the 2012 circular prohibits the charge. Ask for a revised
quotation.

If you have already paid it, ask for a refund on the same grounds. There is no
rule that a charge wrongly collected becomes yours to keep because time passed.

## If they refuse

Thirty days after that written complaint, the RBI Ombudsman is open to you, free
and online. This is an unusually clean complaint to adjudicate: it turns on two
facts, the rate type and the borrower type, both documented in the sanction letter.

## After the loan closes

Two things to chase immediately, because both are routinely forgotten and both
cause problems later: the **No Objection Certificate** and the **return of your
property documents**.

Then check your credit report in about a month. A closed loan still showing as
