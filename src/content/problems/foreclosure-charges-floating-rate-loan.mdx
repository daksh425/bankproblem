Paying off a loan early should be the one transaction a bank cannot complicate.
In practice people are quoted a foreclosure charge of two to four per cent of the
outstanding amount, pay it, and never learn that on most home loans it was not
owed.

## The rule changed on 1 January 2026

There are now two regimes, and which one applies to you depends on **when your
loan was sanctioned or renewed**.

<Callout type="rule" title="Loans sanctioned or renewed on or after 1 January 2026" cite="RBI (Pre-payment Charges on Loans) Directions, 2025, notified 2 July 2025" citeUrl="https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx">
Regulated entities **shall not** levy any pre-payment charge on **floating-rate
loans to individuals for purposes other than business** — with or without a
co-borrower, whether you prepay in part or in full, and with **no minimum
lock-in period**.

The Directions cover commercial banks other than payments banks, co-operative
banks, NBFCs and All India Financial Institutions, and extend protection to
loans to micro and small enterprises.
</Callout>

That is a meaningful widening of the older rule. The 2012 circular applied to
**banks only**; the 2025 Directions bring NBFCs, co-operative banks and AIFIs
inside it, remove any argument about lock-in periods, and reach small business
borrowers for the first time.

<Callout type="warn" title="Loans sanctioned before 1 January 2026">
The older position governs: the **2012 circular**, which prohibits foreclosure
charges and pre-payment penalties on floating-rate term loans sanctioned to
individual borrowers, but which was addressed to **banks**. If your loan predates
2026 and came from an NBFC, you are relying on the sanction letter and the
lender's published schedule of charges rather than on that circular.
</Callout>

## Where the protection still ends

- **Fixed-rate loans.** The lender does carry rate risk here, and a charge is contractual. Read the sanction letter for the percentage and any lock-in.
- **Loans to non-individuals** beyond the micro and small enterprise scope in the Directions — a loan to a larger company, partnership or LLP.
- **Loans to individuals for business purposes**, which are treated separately from the non-business protection. Check the Directions for your lender category.

A dual-rate loan that was fixed for an initial period and has since converted to
floating is worth arguing about: once it is floating, the usual position is that
the prohibition applies.

<Callout type="warn" title="Check two things in the sanction letter">
**The rate type** — fixed or floating — and **the sanction or renewal date**.
Borrowers often know neither, and the bank quoting the charge has no incentive to
clarify. The whole argument turns on those two facts, and both are on the
sanction letter and the latest statement.
</Callout>

## What to do when you are quoted a charge

Ask for the foreclosure quotation **in writing**, itemised. A written quotation
showing a foreclosure charge on a floating-rate individual loan is the only
evidence you need, and banks are noticeably more careful about what they will put
in an email than what a branch will say across a desk.

Then write to the grievance redressal officer with three lines: the loan account
number, that the loan is a floating-rate loan sanctioned to you as an individual
for a non-business purpose, and the instrument that prohibits the charge — the
**Pre-payment Charges on Loans Directions, 2025** if the loan was sanctioned or
renewed on or after 1 January 2026, or the **2012 circular** if it is older and
from a bank. Ask for a revised quotation.

If you have already paid it, ask for a refund on the same grounds. There is no
rule that a charge wrongly collected becomes yours to keep because time passed.

## If they refuse

Thirty days after that written complaint, the RBI Ombudsman is open to you, free
and online. This is an unusually clean complaint to adjudicate: it turns on two
facts, the rate type and the borrower type, both documented in the sanction letter.

## After the loan closes

Two things to chase immediately, because both are routinely forgotten and both
cause problems later: the **No Objection Certificate** and the **return of your
property documents**.
[What to do when the NOC does not arrive](/loans/loan-noc-not-issued-after-closure).

Then check your credit report in about a month. A closed loan still showing as
open is common and it affects your eligibility for the next one.
