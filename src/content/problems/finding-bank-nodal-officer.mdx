Call centres are not built to escalate. They are built to close tickets, and the
fastest close is a reassurance that someone will call you back.

The escalation ladder exists and every bank is required to publish it. You just
have to go and find it rather than asking for it.

## Where it is published

Look on the bank's own website, not on an aggregator. The page is usually linked
from the footer and called one of: **Grievance Redressal**, **Customer Care**,
**Complaints**, or **Customer Grievance Redressal Policy**.

That page must set out the levels, the contact details at each, and the response
time the bank commits to. If you cannot find it, the policy PDF is often linked
from the "Customer Service" or "Regulatory Disclosures" section.

What you are looking for, in order:

- **Level 1 — customer care.** The call centre and the web complaint form. Always start here, because you need the reference number.
- **Level 2 — grievance redressal officer**, often regional.
- **Level 3 — nodal officer**, usually one per zone or region.
- **Level 4 — principal nodal officer**, one per bank, at head office.

<Callout type="warn" title="Always start at Level 1, even when you know it will fail">
The Ombudsman needs evidence that you complained to the bank and that 30 days
passed. Skipping the first rung means skipping the reference number and the
timestamp, and a complaint filed without them gets returned as premature. The
call centre ticket is not useless; it is your proof of when the clock started.
</Callout>

## How long to wait at each level

Banks publish their own turnaround commitments, commonly a few working days at
Level 1 and a week or two further up. Hold them to what they published rather than
to a figure you assume.

In practice: give Level 1 about a week, then move up with the reference number.
There is no benefit in waiting a month at a level that has gone silent.

## What makes an escalation work

The same things that make the original complaint work: the account number, the
dates, the earlier reference number, the rule, and a specific ask.
[The structure to copy](/disputes/writing-a-bank-complaint-that-works).

Add one line that the lower level did not resolve it, with the date. That is what
makes it an escalation rather than a duplicate complaint, and duplicates get
merged and closed.

## Then the Ombudsman

Thirty days from your **first written complaint** — not from the escalation — the
RBI Ombudsman is open. Free, online, no lawyer, and it covers banks, NBFCs and
digital payment participants under one scheme.
