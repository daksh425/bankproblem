The pattern is familiar to most NRIs: everything works for years, then a transfer
fails and the account turns out to be frozen for KYC that was never requested in
a way you noticed.

The notices usually went to an Indian mobile number you stopped using and an email
you no longer check.

## Find out exactly what is missing

Write to the branch and ask for a **specific list**. Not "complete your KYC" —
which document, in what form, with what attestation.

This one question saves the most time. People send a bundle of documents, have
them rejected for the wrong attestation, and repeat the cycle over weeks.

<Callout type="warn" title="Ask which attestation routes the bank accepts">
The usual options are the Indian embassy or consulate, a notary public in your
country of residence, an overseas branch of the same bank, or a correspondent
bank. Banks differ and some branches are stricter than their own policy requires.
Get the accepted list in writing before you pay anyone for an attestation.
</Callout>

## What is normally required

- **Passport** — photo and address pages.
- **Visa, residence permit or work permit** evidencing your status abroad.
- **Overseas address proof** — a utility bill, bank statement or tenancy agreement.
- **PAN**.
- The bank's re-KYC form, signed.

Where the account is still a resident savings account that was never redesignated,
expect the bank to raise that at the same time. It is a reasonable thing for it to
raise.
[What redesignation involves](/nri/resident-account-after-moving-abroad).

## Pushing it along

Submit by the route the bank specified, keep the courier tracking, and ask for
written confirmation of receipt. Then ask for a timeline.

If nothing moves in a fortnight, escalate in writing to the grievance redressal
officer: the account number, the date you submitted, what you sent, and what you
want, which is the account unfrozen.

Thirty days from the written complaint, the RBI Ombudsman will take up a bank's
failure to act on documents validly submitted.
[How to file](/disputes/rbi-ombudsman-how-to-file) — and note you can file from
abroad; the process is online.

## Preventing the next one

This recurs every few years, so make it cheap:

- Keep an **Indian mobile number alive** on the account, even a low-cost one. Almost every bank notice still goes to SMS.
- Use an **email address you actually read** and update it on every account and folio.
- Update the **overseas address** whenever you move, rather than waiting for the bank to ask.
- Put a reminder in for the bank's **KYC cycle**, typically every few years depending on risk category.

And do the same for your demat account and mutual fund folios. They freeze
independently, and usually at a slightly different time, so fixing one does not
fix the others.

If the freeze turns out not to be KYC at all, the cause matters a great deal and
the route out is different.
[The other reasons an account gets frozen](/accounts/bank-account-frozen-without-notice).
