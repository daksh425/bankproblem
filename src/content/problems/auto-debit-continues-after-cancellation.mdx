Cancelling a subscription inside an app and cancelling the payment mandate on
your card are two different actions. People do the first, assume it did the
second, and keep getting charged.

## Three places a recurring charge can live

**A card-on-file mandate with the merchant.** The merchant holds your card
details and charges them on a schedule. Cancelling in the app should stop it. Often
it does not, because the cancellation closed the service and left the billing
agreement alone.

**An e-mandate registered through your bank.** Under the recurring payments
framework, mandates above the threshold require you to approve each debit, and
every e-mandate carries a pre-debit notification. These you can see and cancel in
your bank's app or net banking, usually under "manage mandates".

**A standing instruction on an account** rather than a card.
[That is a slightly different problem](/payments/standing-instruction-not-cancelled).

## Cancel it at the bank, not just the merchant

This is the step people skip. Go to your card issuer's app or net banking, find
the registered mandates list, and revoke the one you want gone. If you cannot
find it, call and ask for the mandate to be cancelled, and insist on a service
request number.

<Callout type="rule" title="You can withdraw a mandate at any time">
A mandate is your instruction to your bank. You are entitled to withdraw it, and
the bank must act on the withdrawal. "Contact the merchant" is not an answer to a
cancellation request made to your bank.
</Callout>

Then tell the merchant in writing as well, so there is no argument later about
whether you cancelled the service.

## If charges went through after you cancelled

A debit taken after a valid cancellation is not a billing dispute with the
merchant. It is an unauthorised transaction on your card, and the rules there are
much more favourable to you.

Report it to the bank promptly, in writing, with the date you cancelled and the
reference number. Reporting within **three working days** of noticing matters: it
is the difference between zero liability and a capped loss.
[The full liability rules and the 10-working-day credit](/fraud/unauthorised-transaction-bank-refund).

## The thing not to do

Do not close the card to stop a charge. It is a real tactic people reach for and
it causes two new problems: a closed card can still have charges routed to its
replacement through the card network's updater service, and closing a card hurts
your utilisation and account age.
[Why that costs you score](/credit-score/credit-score-dropped-after-closing-card).

Kill the mandate instead. It is the clean fix.
