A wrong entry on a credit report costs you real money — a higher rate, a smaller
limit, or a rejection you cannot explain. The dispute process works, but it is
built around the lender rather than around you, and knowing that changes how you
file.

## The rule most people do not know

<Callout type="rule" title="₹100 a day after 30 days" cite="RBI framework on delayed updation of credit information, effective 26 April 2024" citeUrl="https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12556">
Credit bureaus and the lenders that report to them must resolve a credit
information complaint within **30 calendar days**. Beyond that, you are entitled
to **₹100 for each day of delay**. The compensation is shared between the bureau
and the lender according to where the delay actually sat.
</Callout>

The framework also requires the bureau to tell you when a dispute is rejected,
with reasons. A silent rejection is not compliant.

## Why disputes get rejected

Rarely because the entry is actually right. The common causes are mechanical:

- **No documentary evidence attached.** A bureau cannot overrule a lender on your
  say-so. The NOC, the final payment receipt, the closure letter — one document
  does more than any amount of explanation.
- **Filed only with the bureau.** The bureau forwards the dispute and waits. If
  the lender does not respond, the entry stands. Filing with both in parallel is
  the fix.
- **Several errors in one dispute.** Bundling a wrong amount, a wrong date and a
  wrong status invites one blanket rejection. File separately.
- **The entry is on a different bureau.** Lenders do not report uniformly to all
  four. Correcting CIBIL does nothing for the Experian report a lender actually
  pulled.

<Callout type="warn" title="'Settled' is not a neutral word">
In credit reporting, **settled** means the lender accepted less than the full
amount owed. It reads to the next lender as a partial default and can outweigh
years of clean repayment. If you paid in full, the status must be **closed**. Banks
mark this wrongly often enough that it is worth checking every closed loan on
your report.
</Callout>

## Filing it properly

A dispute is a document-matching exercise. Treat it as one.

## When the lender will not budge

Credit information complaints fall squarely within the Reserve Bank – Integrated
Ombudsman Scheme. Once 30 days have passed without resolution, you can file free
of charge, online, against both the bureau and the lender.

In the complaint, state the dispute reference number, the date you filed, the
specific entry, and the compensation now accruing under the RBI framework. The
compensation provision is recent enough that many lenders have not internalised
it, and naming it tends to produce a faster answer than the dispute itself did.
