You cleared the loan. The bank acknowledged it. Three months later your credit
report still lists the account as live, sometimes with the old outstanding balance
sitting there.

It is a reporting failure, not a dispute about whether you paid, and that makes it
one of the more fixable problems on a credit report.

## Why it happens

Lenders report to bureaus in monthly batches. A closure processed on the 28th may
miss that month's upload entirely. One cycle of lag is normal.

Beyond that, the usual causes are a branch closing the account in its own system
without triggering the bureau update, a foreclosure processed through a different
workflow from a scheduled closure, or a small residual amount — a few rupees of
interest, an unreversed charge — keeping the account technically open.

That last one is worth checking first. Ask for a statement and confirm the balance
is genuinely nil, not ₹14.

## What it costs you

Lenders assess what you can afford using the obligations on your report. A repaid
loan still showing a balance inflates those obligations, and your apparent capacity
for a new EMI shrinks accordingly.

This is one of the quieter reasons a strong applicant gets rejected.
[Five things that reject a good-score applicant](/credit-score/loan-rejected-despite-good-credit-score).

## Fixing it

File with the bureau and the lender **at the same time**. The bureau cannot change
what the lender reported; it can only ask. If the lender does not respond, the
entry stands. Filing in parallel removes the round trip, and it is the single
biggest thing that speeds this up.

Attach the NOC or the closure statement. One document does more than any amount
of explanation.
[If you do not have the NOC yet, get it first](/loans/loan-noc-not-issued-after-closure) —
the dispute is much weaker without it.

<Callout type="rule" title="Thirty days, then ₹100 a day" cite="RBI (Credit Information Reporting) Directions, 2025" citeUrl="https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12764">
Bureaus and lenders must resolve a credit information complaint within **30
days**. Beyond that you are entitled to **₹100 for every day of delay**, split
between the bureau and the lender according to where the delay actually sat.
</Callout>

## Check all four

Lenders do not report uniformly to CIBIL, Experian, Equifax and CRIF. A loan
correctly closed on one report can still be open on another, and you will not know
which bureau your next lender pulls.

Fix it everywhere it is wrong.
