Two words appear against a closed loan or card on your credit report, and they
look almost interchangeable. They are not. One is neutral. The other is close to
a default.

## What each one means

**Closed** means the account ended with nothing outstanding. You paid what you
owed and the lender shut the account. This is the normal, good outcome and it
carries no penalty.

**Settled** means the lender accepted less than the full amount and wrote off the
rest. It appears when you negotiated a one-time settlement, or when a lender
closed a long-overdue account for whatever it could recover.

<Callout type="warn" title="A lender reads 'settled' as a partial default">
It tells the next lender that you have, at least once, not repaid in full and
that they took a loss. On many internal scorecards a settled entry is a hard
filter, regardless of what the headline score says. It can outweigh years of
clean repayment.
</Callout>

There is also **written off**, which is worse again: the lender gave up without
recovering anything.

## When it is simply wrong

This is more common than it should be. The usual cases:

- You paid the full amount but the lender's recovery team logged it as a settlement because that is the workflow they were using.
- You paid a final amount that included waived late fees, and the waiver got recorded as a write-off.
- The account belongs to someone with a similar name or an old phone number of yours.

If you paid in full, the status must be **closed**. The evidence that settles it
is the No Objection Certificate or the final payment receipt. One document beats
any amount of explanation.

## Getting it corrected

File the dispute with the bureau and with the lender at the same time. The bureau
cannot overrule the lender on your word alone; it can only ask, and if the lender
does not reply the entry stands. Going to both removes that round trip.

Attach the NOC. File one dispute for this one entry rather than bundling it with
other corrections, because a single weak item invites a blanket rejection of the
lot.

Since April 2024, if it is not resolved within 30 days you are owed ₹100 for
every further day of delay.
[The full dispute process, and why most get rejected](/credit-score/credit-report-error-dispute).

## If you are being offered a settlement now

Think hard before accepting. The call centre will present it as closing the
matter. It does close the collection calls. It also puts a marker on your file
that will affect every loan and card application for years, often more than the
missed payments themselves did.

If you can pay the full outstanding amount over a longer period instead, ask for
that. A late full payment leaves a far better record than a prompt partial one.
