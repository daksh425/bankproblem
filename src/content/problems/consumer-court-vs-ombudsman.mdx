Both routes are open to an ordinary bank customer and neither requires a lawyer to
start. They suit different problems.

## The short version

<div class="tablewrap">

| | RBI Ombudsman | Consumer commission |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost | Free | Fee, scaled to claim value |
| Lawyer | Not needed | Permitted, not required |
| Speed | Usually months | Usually longer |
| Scope | Regulated entities; rule and process breaches | Deficiency of service generally |
| Will it review a lending decision? | No | It can look at how the decision was handled |
| Prerequisite | Complain to the bank, wait 30 days | None, though it helps |

</div>

## Choose the Ombudsman when a rule was broken

It is at its strongest where the facts are documented and an instrument settles
the matter: a reversal that missed the turn-around time, an unauthorised
transaction refund refused, a card closure not processed in seven working days, a
credit report dispute left open past 30 days.

These turn on dates and a circular. The Ombudsman adjudicates them quickly,
frequently by simply putting the question to the bank, and a meaningful share
settle the moment an external body is looking.

It is free, so where it fits there is no reason to start anywhere else.

<Callout type="warn" title="It will not second-guess a commercial decision">
If your complaint is that the bank should have lent to you, or should have offered
better terms, the Ombudsman will decline. That is the lender's call to make.
Reframe it around the **process** — a published commitment not followed, a reason
not conveyed — or take it elsewhere.
</Callout>

## Choose a consumer commission when the loss is larger or broader

Deficiency of service is a wider idea than non-compliance with a circular. A
commission can consider negligence, mis-selling, and consequential loss, and it
can award compensation and costs.

It is the better route when the amount is substantial, when the harm is not
reducible to a missed deadline, or when the Ombudsman has already declined on
scope grounds.

Trade-off: it is slower and it costs something.

## You cannot run both

The Ombudsman will not admit a complaint already pending before a court or
consumer forum on the same cause of action. Pick one.

The practical order for most people: complain to the bank in writing, wait 30 days,
file with the Ombudsman because it is free and fast. If that route closes — on
scope, or after an unsuccessful appeal — the consumer commission is still open.
